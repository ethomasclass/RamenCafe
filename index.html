Menya Etsuko · a small town in Tohoku, Japan · this summer

Where you are

You are spending the summer helping at your grandmother’s ramen counter. There are six seats. She has been running it for forty years and she is not as fast as she was.

Tonight seven people will sit down and talk to you while you make them something.

You cannot lose. Nobody storms out, every bowl gets eaten. The only thing that changes is how much people tell you.

What you do

Nobody reads off a menu. They tell you what kind of evening they are having — “something plain, the way it’s always been” — and you work out what that means.

Every bowl is three clicks: one broth and two toppings. Tip it out and start again as often as you like.

Get somebody’s bowl right and they tell you the thing they were not going to say. Get it wrong and you never find out.

Between customers

When the shop is empty, check your phone. Everyone in this town is arguing about the same things your customers are — the closed school, the empty houses, whose turn it is to be paid for.

Click any underlined term to see what it means. Your Notebook fills in by itself as you cook, and keeps the words you will need afterwards.

You left an evening half-finished.
