# Ramen Talk — how to play

**Open the link. That's it.** Nothing to install, nothing to sign in to.
It works on a school Chromebook, a laptop, an iPad, or a phone turned sideways.

---

## What you're doing

You are spending the summer helping at your grandmother's ramen counter in a
small town in northern Japan. Tonight, eight people sit down and talk to you
while you make them something to eat.

**You cannot lose.** Nobody storms out. Every bowl gets eaten, even a bad one.
There is no score.

The only thing that changes is **how much people tell you.**

---

## The three things you do

### 1. Talk

Every so often you choose what to say. There are always three kinds:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Curious** | Ask a direct question. Gets you facts. |
| **Empathetic** | Say what you think they're feeling. Gets you the personal answer. |
| **Playful** | Make a joke. Lightens the mood, and some people open up when it's light. |

No choice is wrong. Different people respond to different ones — the same
question that gets a long answer out of Kenji makes Etsuko change the subject.

### 2. Cook

Nobody reads off a menu. They tell you what kind of evening they're having and
you work out what that means.

- **One broth** (of four)
- **Two toppings** (of eight)

Read their order at the top of the cooking screen. *"The plainest thing you
have"* is a real instruction. So is *"something rich and a little sweet."*

You can **tip the bowl out** and start again as many times as you like.

### 3. Check your phone

Between customers the shop is empty. Open **Tsubuyaki** and read a few posts.
Everyone in the country is arguing about the same things your customers are.

Some posts have polls. Vote in them — there's no right answer, and you get to
see what everyone else said.

---

## The part that matters

**Every customer has one thing they will only say over a bowl that actually
suited them.**

Get somebody's food right and they tell you the thing they haven't told their
own family. Get it wrong and they say "thank you, that was lovely" and you
never find out it existed.

The closing screen tells you how many you missed.

The order is a real instruction, and sometimes the only clue to it is
something the person said earlier. Listen before you cook. Two students who played the
same game will not have heard the same night.

---

## Buttons

| | |
|---|---|
| **Notebook** | Fills in by itself as you cook. Every broth, every topping, a map, and the notes you'll want afterwards. |
| **Words** | Every term that turned up tonight, explained. Greyed-out ones haven't come up yet. |
| **Aa** | Text size and speed. Set "all at once" if you read fast. |
| **Sound off** | Turns the shop on — the pot, the fan, a car going past. Off until you ask for it. |

**Underlined words** in conversation can be clicked. So can things in the room
when the shop is empty — the calendar, the photo, the shutter across the street.

**To advance:** click, or press space or enter. `Esc` closes any panel.

Your night saves itself. If the tab dies or the period ends, the title screen
offers to carry on where you stopped.
